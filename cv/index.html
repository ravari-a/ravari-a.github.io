Professional Summary

Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Northeastern University working on reinforcement learning and Bayesian inference for human–AI collaboration. Research focuses on learning models of human decision-making and expert knowledge from limited behavioral data, using expert-enabled inference of hidden Markov models, inverse reinforcement learning conditioned on human factors, and multi-agent reinforcement learning under partial observability, with applications in cyber defense, disaster response, and biomedical sciences. Ten peer-reviewed publications (eight first-author) in venues including Artificial Intelligence, IEEE Transactions on Artificial Intelligence, IEEE Control Systems Letters, L4DC, ACC, and ICASSP. Open to research internships and full-time positions for Spring and Summer 2027.

Experience

  • 2022 - Present

    Boston, MA

    Doctoral Researcher
    Northeastern University
    Imani Lab, advised by Prof. Mahdi Imani.
    • Formulated expert-enabled Bayesian inference that models experts as imperfect reinforcement learning agents, incorporating expert-acquired data into the inference of hidden Markov models and biological regulatory networks.
    • Designed an adversarial inverse reinforcement learning algorithm conditioned on three human factors (fatigue, expertise, risk tolerance) to learn operator-specific defense policies, outperforming factor-oblivious baselines on CAGE Challenge 2.
    • Developed methods for learning humans’ implicit perception of complex, unknown environments and for inferring latent task models in human–AI collaboration under uncertainty, evaluated in simulated rescue tasks.
    • Built hybrid probabilistic models of heterogeneous human teams under partial observability that generalize centralized and decentralized models and outperform both at high rationality levels.
    • Developed a kernel-based inverse learning framework that infers individual defenders’ latent cognitive traits (e.g., risk tolerance, curiosity) from limited behavioral data, validated on 108 participants across 26 cybersecurity scenarios; personalized models exceeded 0.8 action-prediction similarity versus at most 0.6 for a group-level RL baseline, and estimated traits more accurately than GPT-4o and an LSTM-encoder baseline.
    • Contributed to posterior-conditioned multi-agent reinforcement learning for adaptive cyber defense, in which decentralized defenders condition on an attack-context belief inferred online, evaluated on the CAGE-4 testbed.
